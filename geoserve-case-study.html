GeoStem

How I designed a maritime dashboard for traders in just 5 days.

Role

Product Designer

Company

Geoserve

Timeline

10 days

Scope

Dashboard · Port Map · Fuel Price · Design System

Context

A small trading desk, losing deals to the clock

Geoserve's traders were losing fuel inquiries to the clock - jumping between Splash, news websites, WhatsApp, and spreadsheets just to answer a single pricing question. I turned that fragmented workflow into a production-ready 0→1 platform in 5 days.

One interface for:

  • Live fuel pricing
  • Port & bunkering data
  • Deal flow
  • Real-time port mapping

The goal wasn't just a better dashboard. It was to design around how traders actually work - faster decisions, fewer tools, and no lost inquiries. And the best part? 5 days to design. 5 days to build.

The challenge

Getting to the real problem, not the relayed version of it

The brief came through the business team with gaps and assumptions that didn't reflect how traders actually worked. Instead of iterating on secondhand requirements, I went directly to the traders to understand the real problems.

That surfaced three key constraints:

01
Time-critical
Slow or stale data could directly cost a trader money.
02
Conflicting KPIs
Senior Traders, Traders, and Sales needed different signals from the same dashboard.
03
No existing system
Every component, token, and pattern had to be built from scratch while shipping the first module.
0-to-1
Full platform designed and shipped from scratch
3-5 min
Per-inquiry response time, down from 15-20 minutes
4 tools
External tools consolidated into one platform

My role

Owning the problem past the design file

I led the research, worked directly with traders, shaped the product scope with the CTO, and defined the two core modules.

When the existing platform couldn't support key features, I took ownership beyond design - proving feasibility, aligning teams, and driving the work through implementation.

Leadership

Turning a "that'll take too long" into a shipped feature

Two features came directly from trader research: distance calculation between ports and fuel pricing by port. Both required new APIs and logic, and the tech team wasn't resourced to build them. They were also hesitant about using AI for development.

So I built working prototypes myself using dummy data, free APIs, and Claude Code to prove feasibility. I took them to the CTO, who backed the approach and assigned a developer to build them.

I also took ownership beyond design - supporting implementation and QA. Both features shipped in five days, with fewer bugs than a typical build.

What this changed about how I work

Prototyping the feature myself wasn't about proving I could code. It was the fastest way to turn a stalled decision into a shipped one - and it's changed how I bring new ideas to engineering ever since: show a working version before asking for buy-in on the idea alone.

Product thinking

Three traders, four decisions

With direct access secured, I ran structured interviews with the core users - a Senior Trader, a Trader, and a Sales lead. Each had a different workflow and different gaps in the existing system.

KT
Karthik
Senior Trader
Needed configurable KPIs - performance signals differ by seniority
Deal pattern analytics to understand which ports close fastest
Port-level map view to see pricing in geographic context
GeoServe-specific pricing separate from external market data
FL
Felicia
Trader
Relied on Sea Searcher for ship tracking - not in GeoStem at all
No visibility of customer credit line when selecting in inquiry - a risk gap
Traders closest to real market rates but couldn't enter price indications
Claims pending not surfaced in follow-ups, buried in emails
SF
Saif
Sales
Ran 4 separate external tools daily alongside GeoStem
Trade Winds for 5-min live pricing, Splash for news, Sea Searcher for ship locations
Wanted broader crude pricing options beyond just Brent and WTI
Performance dashboard valued but fuel-split data too granular for sales role

Four themes shaped the design direction:

01
Tool consolidation
Every user ran 3-5 external tools alongside GeoStem. Significant opportunity to consolidate if depth matched Sea Searcher and Trade Winds.
02
Role-based KPIs
Senior Traders, Traders, and Sales have fundamentally different performance signals. One dashboard view can't serve all three.
03
Trader write access
Price indications could only be entered by analysts, but traders were closest to real market data. A critical workflow gap.
04
Context-rich follow-ups
Follow-ups needed more than a task label - claims status, credit line, and inquiry history needed to be visible in-context.

Working with traders, I defined two core modules:

GeoStem Dashboard - live pricing, port intelligence, and deal pipeline.

Distance Calculator - weather routing, waypoint planning, and structured reports.

Both were built on a shared design system, developed alongside the first module.

What actually shipped

Live fuel price chart

A full-screen TradingView-powered commodity view for Brent, Crude, and Natural Gas, with six key price stats and instant colour-coded deltas.

The view was built directly in Claude using the design system we created, turning the designed patterns into a functional interface.

Interactive global port map

A Leaflet-based port map with a custom dark navy tile treatment. Clicking a port surfaces fuel grades (VLSFO, HSFO, LSMGO), weather conditions, top sellers, and nearby ports - including the distance calculator and per-port fuel pricing, the two features that took a CTO's intervention to get built. An overlays panel adds maritime context - ECA zones, piracy areas, storm routing, and base layer modes.

Systems

Created a design system as skill which can be used for upcoming features

Every token, component, and pattern built for the GeoStem dashboard was designed to outlive it. The same system now powers the Voyage Optimisation Report, plus validation screens and onboarding flows added later - without a single redesign.

Outcome

What changed for the traders

GeoStem replaced 4 fragmented tools - traders stopped opening them entirely. The number that matters most: responding to a fuel inquiry, which used to take 15-20 minutes of checking prices across tools, now takes 3-5 minutes. Deal pipeline visibility, which didn't exist before, is now a single view traders check without switching tabs.

Reflection

What I'd do differently

I ran the trader interviews directly, but in hindsight, I should have brought the Product Manager into those conversations. It would have reduced the back-and-forth, avoided re-explaining findings, and created alignment earlier.
